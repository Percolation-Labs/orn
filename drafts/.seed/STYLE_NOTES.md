# Voice notes (for the assistant, derived from @mrsirsh posts)

Written in first person, conversational but precise. Engineer sharing what they tried, not essayist performing.

Patterns that come up repeatedly:

- Opens with a plain statement of context and what the piece is about. No throat-clearing.
- Frequent use of "I" and "we" (Percolate). The author is in the piece.
- Technical claims get grounded in actual code: repo links, file paths, endpoint names, excerpts.
- Italics for introducing a term once, then normal type after that.
- Blockquotes used once per article for a single emphasised thought, not for every takeaway.
- Sentences end cleanly with a period. Asides go in parentheses or as their own sentence. Never em-dashes.
- Casual sign-off at the end ("give us some stars", "this seems like one to watch"). No formal conclusion.
- Numbers are given as specific numbers when available (four seconds, not "very fast").
- Honest about uncertainty: "it's too early for me to say", "rough numbers, one run each".

Do NOT use:
- Emojis.
- Em-dashes (—). Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- Litotes: "not uncommon", "not bad", "no small feat", "hardly surprising".
- Over-hedging ("perhaps one might argue"). Either say it or don't.
- Meta openings like "In this article I will discuss...". Get straight to the content.
- Headers as the primary structural device. Paragraphs first, headers sparingly.
